1Taking Turns

What to do at home

Board games, card games, or toss a ball. Take turns talking at dinner. Pass the salt.

Words to use
“My turn” “Your turn” “First you, then me.” “You waited so patiently!”

2Flexibility

What to do at home

Make a small change with a warning. Offer two real choices. Nudge the order of a routine. Model “no big deal.”

Words to use
“In two minutes we’ll switch.” “Which one do you want?” “Oops — no big deal.”

3Camaraderie

What to do at home

Greet at the door. One dinner question. Cheer + high-fives. Turn toward the speaker — do not force eye contact.

Words to use
“What was one good thing today?” “Nice job!” “High five!” “Hi.”
THE GOLDEN RULE
Celebrate every small step. Catch them doing it right and say so — that is how skills grow. Reinforce the skill. Never punish the miss.

Flip side · How we talk · One change for seven days

Autism-friendly communication

1Wait 10

Name. One sentence. Then 8–10 seconds of real silence. Repeating resets the clock.

2Name first

Say their name. Pause. Then the message. Never “look at me.”

3Say less

One idea. Literal words. “Paddle, then car.” — not a long hint.

4Comment, don’t quiz

Share a thought: “That paddle is yellow.” Tests raise pressure.

5Show it

First → Then. Two choices they can point to. A timer they can see.

6Honor every attempt

Word, gesture, AAC, body turn, a wave. If it meant something, answer it.

LISTENING ≠ STARING
We do not force eye contact. Teach “look toward the person” and “turn your body toward them.” A nod, “mm,” or AAC button still counts as a turn.
Reinforce the skill

Never punish the miss. Name it: “You waited.” “You switched.” “Nice hi.”

Parents carry it

You are the change agent. Skills stick when family delivers them (Stokes & Baer, 1977).

Generalize on purpose

Same words on court and at dinner. New person, new place — on purpose.

Teach in real life

Pickleball is the classroom. Naturalistic teaching — not a worksheet.
